YOGA INSTRUCTOR TEACHING TIPS

CUEING EFFECTIVELY
- Use anatomical cues: "press your femur back" rather than vague directions
- Combine action cues with breath: "inhale to lengthen, exhale to fold deeper"
- Use imagery: "imagine your spine is a tall mountain" to help students visualize
- Give both physical and energetic cues for a complete experience
- Avoid over-cueing — silence allows students to feel into the pose

SEQUENCING A CLASS
- Always begin with centering and breath awareness
- Warm up the body gradually — start with gentle movements before deeper poses
- Build peak poses progressively — prepare the body with related poses first
- Counter-pose after deep backbends with forward folds and vice versa
- Always end with Savasana for at least 5 minutes
- Follow the arc: warm up → standing poses → peak pose → cool down → savasana

WORKING WITH BEGINNERS
- Demonstrate poses before students try them
- Offer multiple modifications for every pose
- Remind students to never push through pain
- Focus on alignment over depth
- Encourage use of props (blocks, straps, blankets)
- Create a safe, non-competitive environment

COMMON STUDENT MISTAKES BY BODY PART
Knees: Locking, collapsing inward, placing weight on knee joint in Tree pose
Lower Back: Overarching, rounding, tucking too aggressively
Neck: Straining, crunching in backbends, tensing in inversions
Shoulders: Creeping toward ears, collapsing in Downward Dog
Wrists: Not spreading fingers, putting all weight in heel of hand

PROPS AND WHEN TO USE THEM
- Blocks: Bring the floor closer in standing and seated poses
- Straps: Extend reach in forward folds and binds
- Blankets: Cushion joints, support hips in seated poses
- Bolsters: Support body in restorative poses
- Wall: Aid balance in standing poses and inversions

INJURY AWARENESS
- Always ask students about injuries before class
- Offer alternatives for common issues: bad knees, tight hamstrings, wrist pain
- Never adjust a student without asking permission first
- Know when to refer students to medical professionals
- Knee issues: avoid deep squats, use blocks, keep micro-bend in knees
- Wrist issues: use fists or forearms instead of flat palms
- Lower back issues: keep knees bent in forward folds, avoid deep twists
